Comparison with the IEA energy Sankey (iea.org/sankey)

The IEA shows a total energy supply (TES) of 1,892 PJ for Sweden 2023 vs. 1,976 PJ here. The two agree almost perfectly where conventions coincide; the differences are accounting conventions, not data:

  • Per fuel, IEA vs. this chart (PJ): biofuels & waste 573.5 vs. 573.5 (identical); hydro 238.3 vs. 238.5; wind + solar + other 177.1 vs. 176.8; oil 373.5 vs. 367.5; gas 30.1 vs. 28.2.
  • Nuclear: IEA imputes a fixed 33% reactor efficiency (→ 529 PJ primary); Eurostat reports the actual Swedish reactor heat, ~36% (→ 485 PJ here). Both correspond to the same 48.5 TWh of electricity.
  • Electricity exports: the IEA headline TES nets out net exports (−103 PJ); this chart draws them as an explicit flow instead. IEA’s own by-source total is 1,983 PJ — within 0.3% of this chart.
  • Coal: IEA reports supply (62 PJ, incl. stock changes and the steelworks coke/blast-furnace-gas chain); this chart shows use (34 PJ) with the remainder inside “Own use & losses”.

Methodology & assumptions

  • Layout and end-use efficiencies follow LLNL conventions: residential & commercial 65%, industry 49%, transport 21% (“energy services” vs. “rejected energy”).
  • CHP fuel inputs are split between the electricity and district-heat boxes in proportion to each fuel’s gross electricity vs. heat output.
  • “Commercial & services” includes public services, agriculture, forestry and fishing.
  • Ambient heat (73.2 PJ): 9.9 PJ enters district heating via large heat pumps (Eurostat); the 63.3 PJ captured by building heat pumps is reported by Eurostat under “non-specified” and is allocated here 80% residential / 20% commercial.
  • “Industrial waste heat & other” = Eurostat fuel class X9900 feeding district heating (flue-gas condensation, excess industrial heat).
  • “Coal & peat” includes manufactured gases (coke-oven/blast-furnace gas) and peat. “Biomass” includes renewable municipal waste and transport biofuels; non-renewable waste is shown separately.
  • Refinery/energy-branch own use appears as “Own use & losses”. Non-energy use (feedstocks, lubricants, bitumen) is shown as a separate terminal. International aviation is included in transport; marine bunkers are excluded (Eurostat convention).

Comparison with the IEA energy Sankey (iea.org/sankey)

The IEA shows a total energy supply (TES) of 15,844 PJ for Japan (calendar 2023) vs. 17,558 PJ here (METI, fiscal 2023). The ≈1,714 PJ gap is fully explained by four convention differences — neither figure is wrong:

  • Calorific basis (≈ +970 PJ): METI books all fuels at gross calorific value (HHV); IEA uses net (LHV). This inflates gas ≈ +354 PJ, oil ≈ +409 PJ, coal ≈ +204 PJ relative to IEA. Japanese national statistics are always a few percent above IEA figures for this reason.
  • Renewable electricity accounting (≈ +916 PJ): METI converts hydro/solar/wind electricity to “primary equivalent” by dividing by the average fossil-plant efficiency (implied factor 41.8%: IEA hydro 271 PJ actual electricity vs. 648 PJ here). IEA counts these 1:1. Partly offset by nuclear (METI actual ~38.5% heat → 724 PJ vs. IEA fixed 33% → 917 PJ; −193 PJ) and geothermal (IEA imputes 10% efficiency; −93 PJ).
  • Recovered energy (≈ +273 PJ): METI counts recovered industrial waste heat and electricity (未活用エネルギー) as primary supply; IEA does not.
  • Fiscal vs. calendar year plus LHV conversion of biomass/waste: the remainder (≈ −150 PJ).

Consequence of the substitution method: ≈0.9 EJ of the “rejected energy” leaving the electricity box is an accounting artifact for hydro/solar/wind rather than physical waste heat — the same convention LLNL uses in its U.S. chart.

Methodology & assumptions

  • Layout and end-use efficiencies follow LLNL conventions: residential & commercial 65%, industry 49%, transport 21%.
  • “Electricity generation” merges utility power (事業用発電, incl. pumped-storage losses) and auto-producers (自家用発電 — includes most rooftop/on-site solar PV).
  • “Steam & heat supply” merges industrial auto-steam boilers (自家用蒸気, 933 PJ) and district heat-supply business (熱供給, 32 PJ); its output flows mostly back to manufacturing.
  • “Industrial” = manufacturing + agriculture, mining and construction (#610000+#620000); “Commercial & services” = 業務他 (#650000).
  • “Waste & recovered energy” = 未活用エネルギー (refuse-fired power, waste tires/plastics, recovered industrial heat).
  • City gas is shown inside the natural-gas band; the ≈163 PJ of LPG/refinery feed blended into city gas is allocated proportionally to the oil band’s deliveries.
  • Non-energy use (naphtha and other feedstocks, 1,234 PJ) is a separate terminal. “Own use & losses” bundles energy-industry own use (coke ovens, refineries), stock changes and the statistical difference.
  • Nuclear, hydro, geothermal and PV inputs use METI primary-equivalent accounting (thermal input for nuclear/geothermal; generated electricity for hydro/PV/wind).
